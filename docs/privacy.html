Privacy guidance

POF hides amounts and which notes back a certificate. It does not hide everything, and some of what it does not hide is on chain at the moment you fund the vault. This page lists what each step reveals, then the habits that help.

Two consequences follow directly:

  1. The vault isolates the nullifier key, not your identity. The vault keeps the nullifier key of your main ring away from the Verifier. It does not hide that the vault is yours: it is linked to the wallet in public, by its registration, by every shield (a public deposit from the wallet naming the vault) and by the plain transfer that funds the vault key's fees. Shielding from a fresh wallet that has no link to you is the only way to fund a vault with no wallet link.
  2. For an X-handle or badge certificate, the chain does not name the vault, but timing and amounts can. If a vault receives a public deposit of 1,200 SOL and a handle gets a "≥ 1,000 SOL" certificate a little later, an observer can pair them, the more easily the rarer deposits of that size are.

Habits that help#

Certify from the vault, never from your main ring. That is the client's default. The Verifier sees the nk of the ring you certify from; with it, it could follow when that ring's deposits are spent and read its merge outputs.

Wait between funding and certifying. The Verifier refuses notes younger than 150 slots. That is a floor against the crudest pairing, not a privacy guarantee. Fund the vault well before you need the certificate, and let other deposits of similar size land in between.

Prefer a private transfer into the vault over a public deposit. A deposit from a public balance shows the amount. A private Rings transfer from another ring into the vault hides it (its output has a random blinding and an encrypted amount). When you deposit publicly, avoid amounts that sit just above one tier.

Let the relayer submit handle and badge certificates. The client does this by default: the relayer is the payer, so no wallet appears in the certificate, its event or its log entry. How you pay the relayer decides what remains:

  • With relay credits (when credits are available). You buy credits ahead of time by a private transfer from the vault into the relayer's house ring, and each certificate later spends one. Nothing is paid next to the certificate and no amount carries a tag. The transfer is still visible as a transaction the vault signed (amount and recipient hidden). Credits are blind-signed (RSA blind signatures, checked in the page before they are kept), so when a credit is spent the relayer cannot tell which purchase paid for it. It still sees that the vault bought credits.
  • Paying at certify time. The fee is a withdrawal from the vault, a spend signed by the vault key that lands shortly before the relayer's certify. Someone watching both can pair the vault with the certificate by timing.
  • From your wallet. That names the wallet as payer; the client asks for explicit consent before it does it.

Revoke first, then move. Spending notes behind a live certificate lets anyone holding the kit revoke it by spend, and that reveals a nullifier that matches a transaction signed by the vault. For a handle or badge certificate, revoke it with the controller key first (it reveals nothing), wait for confirmation, then spend.

Use a badge for a one-off deal. A badge key is made fresh for one certificate and links to nothing else. Hand the counterparty the badge address; they check it live; you revoke or let it expire.

Keep the kit file private. The exported kit contains each certificate's salts and nullifiers (which can revoke it and, after a spend, link it) and the openings and badge secrets, sealed under a key derived from the vault seed (never from nk, which the Verifier sees). If your wallet signs non-deterministically, the file also carries the random vault key under your passphrase: it is then the only way to recover the vault on another device.

Never sign the vault message anywhere else. Its signature is the vault's spending key. The message says so, and names its domain.

Know what the operator sees. The Verifier operator can link subject, vault and funding wallet for each certificate it signs, and the host sees request IP addresses. The code stores no amount, opening or nk; it stores the log (no amounts), the keeper's sealed pairs, and rate-limit counters named by a keyed hash of the client address per window (swept by the keeper). Phase 2 removes the operator's view of amounts and nk (Phase 2); it does not remove the host's view of your IP. Use the network path you trust.

What still links, in one list#

Even when every habit above is followed:

  • the vault and the wallet, in public, through the vault's registration, its shields and its fee funding;
  • the vault's activity: its private transfers are visible as transactions the vault signed (amounts and recipients hidden);
  • subject, vault and wallet, for the operator: the Verifier learns them together at attest time, and the relayer sees which vaults buy credits (not which certificate each credit later pays for);
  • a certificate and its vault, in public, after a revocation by spend: the revealed nullifier matches a spend the vault signed. Revoke first, then move, and this one does not happen.
