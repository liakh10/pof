Proof of Funds

A live, public certificate that a subject holds at least a tier of SOL or USDC in a Helius Privacy Ring. It never shows the amount, and it is revoked on chain when the notes behind it are spent.

The problem#

On a public chain, the way to prove you can pay is to show the wallet, and with it the whole history. Finance solved the same problem long ago with a proof of funds letter: a trusted party states that a client holds at least a sum, without handing over the statement. Since Helius Privacy Rings went live, balances inside a ring are encrypted. That makes a proof of funds both possible and needed: the balance can no longer be shown by pointing at it.

The certificate#

A certificate is a program account ["cert", subject_hash, [asset]]. Its subject is a Solana wallet, an X handle (stored as a hash) or a fresh badge key. It records the asset, the tier (SOL: 1 to 10,000; USDC: 100 to 1,000,000, in powers of ten), the slots it was issued and expires at, and the salted hashes of the nullifiers of the 1 to 8 notes behind it. It never records an amount. Anyone can read it; other programs gate on it with one CPI, require_certificate(subject_hash, asset, min_tier); on-chain Rooms and Telegram and Discord bots gate communities on it.

How it is issued#

  1. The holder's browser derives a vault ring from one wallet signature, registers it and shields funds into it.
  2. It picks the fewest notes, each at least 150 slots old, that reach the tier, and sends their openings, the vault's nullifier key and a disclosure statement signed by the vault key (and by the subject key for a wallet or badge) to the Verifier.
  3. The Verifier recomputes each commitment with the Rings hasher, re-hashes the indexer's Merkle path to a root the tree account holds on chain, derives each nullifier and proves it unspent (no nullifier PDA, and a non-inclusion path to an on-chain nullifier root), checks the sum reaches the tier and that no note backs another live certificate. It then signs 240 bytes: domain, program id, subject, asset, tier, the tree root and its slot, the note count, the hash of the sorted salted nullifier hashes, issue and expiry slots, payer, controller, nonce.
  4. It appends the attestation to a public transparency log (RFC 9162 Merkle hashing, signed tree heads anchored on chain with anchor_log, checked by witnesses anyone can run) and returns the salts and nullifiers to the holder as a revoke kit.
  5. certify rebuilds the 240 bytes on chain and checks them against an Ed25519-program instruction by the active Verifier key, within 300 slots of issue. For an X handle or a badge, a relayer submits it, so the holder's wallet signs nothing public.

How it dies#

Every Rings spend creates a nullifier PDA. Whoever holds a disclosed note's salt and nullifier (the keeper, the holder) calls revoke_spent: the program recomputes the salted hash and checks the spend from the Rings accounts alone, either the PDA or a membership path in the nullifier tree to an on-chain root. The certificate is revoked at that slot and the pair becomes public. The controller can revoke at any time without revealing anything; anyone can close a certificate after its expiry slot.

What you trust#

  • The Verifier sees the disclosed amounts and the vault's nullifier key for one request, and can link subject, vault and funding wallet. The code keeps none of it; every signature is in the public log with the hash of the code that made it. A false attestation is detectable, not impossible.
  • Revocation on spend needs the salts. Only the holder and the registered keepers (each sent its own sealed copy) have them, so it depends on a keeper running. Certificates are issued for 216,000 slots by default and renewed on the same notes, which bounds the damage; the status page shows the keeper's last run.
  • The admin can pause, rotate the Verifier key after a delay counted in slots, set the fee under a hard cap, and, as upgrade authority, upgrade the program.
  • Helius: the Rings programs, the tree, and the indexer (its paths are re-checked against on-chain roots; note creation slots are taken from it). Rings is new and its SDK is alpha.
  • X and fxtwitter for the handle check.
  • Control is not ownership: borrowed funds certify, until they move.
  • Not anonymity: deposits are public, so timing and amounts can pair a vault with a certificate.

Fees#

A fee per certify and renew (0.01 SOL at launch, capped at 0.25 SOL by the program) to the Treasury; the certificate's rent is returned at expiry. A payer holding 1,000,000 $POF in its canonical token account pays no fee. The Treasury's counters are public; its surplus goes to the dev key in Config.

Phase 2#

A Groth16 circuit over the same Poseidon proves membership, non-spending, the tier and an escrow of each nullifier to a revocation key, so the program checks the statement itself and nobody sees an amount or a nullifier key. In development in zk/ and under internal review: certify_zk is switched off on chain until its open Critical and High findings are fixed and re-reviewed and a public setup ceremony has run.
