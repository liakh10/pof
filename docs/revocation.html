The three ways a certificate ends, what each one reveals, and who can do it.

Revocation

A certificate ends in one of these ways. Each one is an instruction anyone can check on chain, and each one emits an event.

howwhowhenwhat becomes public
revoke_spentanyone holding a disclosed note's salt and nullifiera disclosed note is spent on the Rings programthat note's salt and nullifier (status 1)
revoke_by_holderthe certificate's controller, signing the transactionany timenothing beyond the revocation (status 2)
revoke_signedanyone (normally the relayer) submitting the controller's signed revoke statementany time, until the statement's valid_until slotthe same as revoke_by_holder (status 2)
expireanyoneafter expiry_slot (or during the wind-down)nothing; the account closes and its rent goes back to its payer
void_by_keyanyoneafter the admin revoked the Verifier key that signed it (revoke_verifier_key)that its signing key was revoked (status 4, voided)

A certificate whose status byte is still "live" but whose expiry_slot has passed reads as expired everywhere (the program's gate, the SDK, the API) before anyone calls expire.

revoke_spent: proving a spend from chain state#

The certificate stores, for each disclosed note, h = sha256("pof:null:" ‖ salt ‖ nullifier). Whoever calls revoke_spent(index, salt, nullifier, membership?) shows one pair. The program recomputes h, requires it to equal the stored hash at index, and then requires a proof that nullifier is spent on the Rings pool program (ring1GaLCuJnkUMVkX2JeZLTTX17wKsV4fZmPVzXANW), by one of two paths (CONTRACT §5):

(a) The nullifier PDA. Every Rings spend creates the account PDA(["nullifier", tree, nullifier]) under the pool program: 11 bytes, first byte 8. The program checks the account at that address exists, is owned by the pool program, is 11 bytes and starts with 8. No PDA has ever been closed on mainnet (RINGS.md §3), so this is the normal path.

(b) The nullifier tree. The pool may later close nullifier PDAs once their batch is folded into the indexed nullifier tree (height 40). Then the caller passes membership = { leaf_index, next_value, path }: the leaf Poseidon(nullifier, next_value) and its siblings must hash to one of the 100 nullifier roots stored at offset 17,232 of the tree account. About 64,000 compute units in one transaction with the Poseidon syscall (64,626 measured for a real 16-level mainnet path).

(b') The staged path. A full 40-level path does not fit a legacy transaction, so it can also be written in steps: open_path creates the revoker's own buffer account ["path", revoker, certificate] with the nullifier, its next value, its leaf index and the first 20 siblings; stage_path adds the other 20; revoke_spent_staged(index, salt) hashes all 40 levels to a root, requires it to be one of the 100 nullifier roots the tree account holds at that moment, checks the salted hash, revokes, and closes the buffer (its rent back to the revoker). Each step is a legacy transaction (about 22,600, 5,300 and 68,400 compute units). Only the revoker writes its buffer; close_path drops it. It was tested against a real mainnet path.

An absent or closed PDA is never read as "unspent"; there is no path that trusts a statement from the Verifier or from us.

After the revocation, revealed_index, revealed_salt and revealed_nullifier are set on the certificate and in the Revoked event (with path 0 for the PDA, 1 for the tree). Anyone can then check that the revealed nullifier is the one the Rings program recorded.

Who holds the pairs#

  • The holder. The Verifier's answer carries every salt and nullifier. The client saves them in the local revoke kit, which can be exported to a file. With the kit, the holder can revoke without us, from any wallet (the caller pays only the transaction fee).
  • The registered keepers. The Verifier seals the same pairs to each keeper listed in verifier/keepers.json (ephemeral X25519, HKDF-SHA256, AES-256-GCM) and publishes the envelopes at GET /api/keeper/escrow; each keeper opens only its own. An independent keeper needs nothing from our servers but that listing (a mirror works): verifier/scripts/witness-keeper.mjs reads it, checks each nullifier PDA on its own RPC and sends revoke_spent with its own key.
  • The project's keeper also keeps its own sealed copy (AES-256-GCM, the Blob key name as associated data). Each scan opens every kit, reads its certificate, drops kits whose certificate is gone, revoked, replaced or long expired, and for each live one checks whether any disclosed nullifier is spent: first the PDA, then the indexer's answer re-checked against the on-chain nullifier roots. The first spent note is shown to revoke_spent. At most 10 revocations per scan, so a burst is spread over a few scans.

Nobody else can revoke a live certificate by spend, because nobody else knows a salt. That is deliberate: if salts were public, anyone could link a certificate to the later spend of its notes even after the holder revoked it first (see below). The cost is that revocation on spend depends on at least one registered keeper running (Trust points, item 4). Each registered keeper sees every disclosed nullifier and so could link a certificate to a later spend; a keeper is trusted with that and with nothing else (it cannot revoke an unspent certificate).

Before launch the keeper cron is off. Its health (last run, in slots) is on the status page.

What a revocation by spend reveals#

A revealed nullifier is the nullifier of a real spend, and every Rings spend is a transaction signed by the ring owner's Ed25519 key. So a revocation by spend links the certificate to the ring that spent, which for a vault is the vault owner key, and from the vault's registration and funding deposits, to the wallet that funded it.

For a wallet certificate that link is already public (the subject message names the ring, and the subject is the wallet). For an X-handle or badge certificate it is the one link the certificate otherwise never makes.

Revoke first, then move. revoke_by_holder reveals no salt and no nullifier. A holder who wants to spend the notes behind a handle or badge certificate and keep the handle unlinked should revoke it with the controller key first, wait for that transaction to confirm, and only then spend.

revoke_by_holder#

The controller signs revoke_by_holder. For a wallet certificate the controller is the wallet; for a badge it is the badge key (kept in the sealed kit); for an X handle it is the key the client rebuilds from the vault seed. Status becomes 2, the revoked slot is set, the Treasury's live count and live tier sum go down.

revoke_signed: a holder's revoke with no wallet in it#

For a handle or badge certificate, signing revoke_by_holder as a transaction would need the controller key to hold SOL for the fee, and whoever funds it appears in public. revoke_signed avoids that. The controller signs, with ed25519, only these 156 bytes (CONTRACT §5):

"POF-REVOKE1" (11) ‖ program id (32) ‖ certificate address (32) ‖ subject hash (32) ‖ asset (1) ‖
nullset_hash (32) ‖ issued_slot (u64) ‖ valid_until (u64)

Anyone may submit them in an Ed25519 instruction followed by revoke_signed(valid_until), and pay the fee; the relayer does it at POST /api/relay/revoke after checking the signature against the certificate's controller. The statement names the certificate's own values, so it cannot revoke a renewed or remade certificate, and it is refused once the slot is past valid_until (the client suggests the current slot plus 1,500), so a signed revoke cannot be held back and used later.

void_by_key: when a Verifier key is revoked#

If a Verifier key leaks, or is retired after a rotation, the admin calls revoke_verifier_key(key) (up to 4 revoked keys are kept in Config). From that slot the key signs nothing the program accepts, and every certificate it signed fails require_certificate and Rooms at once, whether or not anyone has touched it yet. Anyone may then call void_by_key on each such certificate to mark it voided (status 4) and update the Treasury's counters. A holder whose certificate was voided this way certifies again with the current key; the notes are unaffected.

expire#

After expiry_slot, anyone may call expire. The account closes and its rent goes back to the certificate's payer: the wallet for a wallet certificate, the relayer for a relayed one. During a wind-down (close_begin), anyone may expire any certificate at once.

A subject can be certified again over a certificate that is revoked or past its expiry. The new certify must carry a newer attestation; the old account's rent goes back to its previous payer first.

Rooms follow the certificate#

A pof_gate member stays only while its certificate is live, unexpired, of the room's asset and at least the room's tier, and has the same controller. When any of that stops being true, anyone may evict the member; the program reads the certificate account directly (no CPI), so a closed certificate evicts too. The bots remove the member from the chat on their sweep after launch.
