Proof of Funds

Abstract#

Proof of Funds (POF) issues a public, on-chain certificate that a subject holds at least a tier of SOL or USDC in a Helius Privacy Ring. A subject is a Solana wallet, an X handle, or a fresh badge key. The certificate carries the tier (for example "at least 1,000 SOL"), never an amount. It stays live only while the notes behind it are unspent: when one of them is spent, anyone holding that note's revocation pair can prove the spend from chain state, and the program marks the certificate revoked at that slot.

The protocol has five parts. The holder's browser picks the fewest shielded notes that reach the tier and sends their openings to the Verifier. The Verifier recomputes every commitment, checks that each one is in the Rings state tree under a root the tree account holds, derives every nullifier and checks that none is spent, checks that the sum reaches the tier, and signs a fixed 240-byte attestation. The pof program rebuilds those 240 bytes on chain, checks the Verifier's ed25519 signature, and writes the certificate. The transparency log records every attestation the Verifier signs in an append-only Merkle log whose signed head is anchored on chain. The keeper watches the disclosed nullifiers and calls revoke_spent when one appears; the holder keeps the same revocation pairs, so revocation does not depend on us.

Version 1 has one trusted party for privacy: the Verifier sees the amounts of the notes you disclose and the nullifier key of the ring they sit in, for the length of one request. It keeps neither. The program does not trust the Verifier with revocation: a spend is proven from the Rings program's own accounts. Phase 2 replaces the Verifier's view with a zero-knowledge proof.

What is private and what is not#

who can see it
The subject (wallet, or the hash of an X handle, or a badge key)everyone (an X handle is stored as a hash, but anyone can hash a handle and compare)
The asset and the tiereveryone
The slots it was issued at, expires at, was revoked ateveryone
How many notes back it (1 to 8)everyone
The payer (the wallet itself, or the relayer for handles and badges) and the controller keyeveryone
The amounts of the disclosed notesthe Verifier, during one request; nobody else
The ring the notes sit in, and its nullifier keythe Verifier, during one request; nobody else
Which notes back itthe Verifier (during the request) and the registered keepers (as salted nullifiers they watch); nobody else while it is live; one note becomes public if the certificate is revoked by a spend
The rest of the ring's balance and historynobody (POF never sees notes you do not disclose)
The wallet that funded a vault ringeveryone, on chain, from the deposit itself (see Privacy guidance)

POF is not anonymity. A certificate is as private as the subject you choose and the way you fund the ring behind it.
