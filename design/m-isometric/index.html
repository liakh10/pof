How it works

Five movements, one signature you can read.

The holder's browser does the choosing, the Verifier does the checking, the program does the writing, and the chain does the remembering. Nobody can edit a certificate, including us.

  1. 01 / 05

    Shield

    Move SOL or USDC into your own POF vault, a separate Privacy Ring made from one wallet signature. The money stays yours and stays shielded.

    The deposit
    public: wallet, amount, vault
    After the deposit
    private

  2. 02 / 05

    Disclose

    Pick a tier, from at least 1 SOL to at least 10,000, and a subject: this wallet, an X handle, or a fresh badge that names nobody. Your browser picks the fewest notes that reach the tier and opens only those.

    Notes
    1 to 8, all in tree 0
    Each note
    at least 150 slots old

  3. 03 / 05

    Attest

    The Verifier recomputes every commitment, checks that each one is in the Rings state tree, that none is spent, and that the sum reaches the tier. It keeps nothing, and signs a fixed 240-byte attestation.

    Attestation
    240 bytes, ed25519
    Kept by the Verifier
    nothing

  4. 04 / 05

    Certify

    The pof program rebuilds those 240 bytes on chain, checks the Verifier's signature, and writes the certificate. The transparency log has already recorded the attestation, in an append-only Merkle log whose signed head is anchored on chain.

    Log leaf
    377 bytes, no amounts
    Valid for
    about 30 days, renewable

  5. 05 / 05

    Revoke

    The keeper watches the notes behind every certificate. Spend one and the certificate is revoked on chain, with the slot, for everyone at once. The holder keeps the same revocation pairs, so revocation does not depend on us.

    Instruction
    revoke_spent
    Proven from
    chain state

What a certificate says

A minimum. Never an amount.

It proves

  • The disclosed notes exist in the Rings tree and were unspent at the slot it names.
  • Together they reach the tier: at least 1,000 SOL means at least 1,000 SOL.
  • The subject signed for this vault, so the tier is bound to it.
  • It is still true now: a spend revokes it on chain.

It does not prove

  • How much you hold in total, or anything about the rest of your Ring.
  • That the money was never borrowed. Revocation catches the return, not the loan.
  • Anything about the past. It starts at the slot it was certified.
  • Anonymity. A wallet subject names the wallet; choose a badge to name nobody.
