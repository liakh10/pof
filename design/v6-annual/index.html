01

Problem

On public chains, the usual way to show you can pay is to show the wallet.

Proof of funds letter. A routine instrument in finance: a bank states that a client holds at least a sum, for a counterparty who needs to know the deal can close. Property, acquisitions, OTC trades.

Showing the wallet proves the balance and gives away everything else: the full history, every counterparty, the next move. Traders, funds and people who negotiate deals keep being asked to make that trade.

A shielded pool changes the default. Inside a Helius Privacy Ring, amounts and assets are encrypted, so a balance is no longer public, and it can no longer be shown by pointing at it. The question becomes how to prove a fact about it, "at least this much", without reopening the rest.

02

Proof

A minimum, never an amount. Five parts, one signature you can read.

Tiers are coarse by design. Powers of ten, 1 to 10,000 SOL and 100 to 1,000,000 USDC, so a tier says little beyond what it is asked to say.

The holder's browser picks the fewest shielded notes that reach the tier and sends their openings to the Verifier. The Verifier recomputes every commitment, checks each one sits in the Rings state tree and is unspent, checks the sum reaches the tier, and signs a fixed 240-byte attestation.

The pof program rebuilds those 240 bytes on chain, checks the Verifier's ed25519 signature and writes the certificate. The transparency log records every attestation the Verifier signs. The keeper watches the disclosed nullifiers and revokes the certificate when one appears.

It proves

  1. The disclosed notes exist in the Rings tree and were unspent at the slot it names.
  2. Together they reach the tier: at least 1,000 SOL means at least 1,000 SOL.
  3. The subject signed for this vault, so the tier is bound to it.
  4. It is still true now: a spend revokes it on chain.

It does not prove

  1. How much you hold in total, or anything about the rest of your Ring.
  2. That the money was never borrowed. Revocation catches the return, not the loan.
  3. Anything about the past. It starts at the slot it was certified.
  4. Anonymity. A wallet subject names the wallet; choose a badge to name nobody.

03

Revocation

Moving the money is what ends the certificate.

No PDA has ever been closed. Every Rings spend creates a nullifier account under the pool program. The program checks that account exists, so a spend is proven from Rings' own state, not from our word.

A certificate binds the exact notes it was built from, by salted hashes of their nullifiers. Whoever holds a note's revocation pair can show the spend: the program recomputes the hash, finds the nullifier account on the Rings pool program, and marks the certificate revoked at that slot.

The keeper does this for every certificate, and the holder keeps the same pairs, so revocation does not depend on us. The holder can also revoke at any time, and anyone can close a certificate once its expiry slot has passed.

04

Transparency log

Every signature the Verifier makes is public, ordered and permanent.

No amounts, no openings, no nullifiers. The attestation carries a salted nullset hash, which cannot be tested against a nullifier without the salts.

The log does not make trust in the Verifier unnecessary. It makes a false attestation impossible to withdraw quietly, and a signature made outside the log stands out.

One entry per attestation, appended before the answer leaves the Verifier, in an append-only Merkle log whose signed head is anchored on chain. Your browser can check any entry against it: the leaf, the signature, the trusted key, the path to the root.

06

Trust points

Everything POF asks you to trust, stated first and plainly.

Phase 2. Replaces the Verifier's view with a zero-knowledge proof. Until then, version 1 has one trusted party for privacy, and this list says what it sees.

  1. The Verifier sees the notes you discloseAmounts and the vault's nullifier key, for one request. It keeps neither.
  2. The Verifier's signing key and the program adminOne key at a time, held in the program's Config. A new key takes over only after 216,000 slots, in public.
  3. Helius: the Rings programs, the tree and the indexerPOF relies on their programs and state tree; each fact it uses is checked against mainnet reads.
  4. Revocation on spend depends on whoever holds the revoke kitsThe keeper and the holder both hold the pairs, and certificates are short-lived.
  5. The X handle check relies on XA handle is linked by posting a one-time code, read through a public API. The chain holds only its hash.
  6. A certificate proves control, not ownershipBorrowed funds count. Revocation catches the move, not a lender who leaves them in place.
  7. TimingA public deposit and a quick certify can be paired. The Verifier refuses notes younger than 150 slots.
  8. ScreenshotsA picture of a card proves nothing. The account is the only source of truth.
  9. Our hostingVercel functions and Vercel Blob. The host sees request metadata, like any host.
  10. Open findingsAn internal review, each finding listed with a failing test until it passes.
