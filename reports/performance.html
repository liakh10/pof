Performance and load

What the public surfaces cost under load: the read API (/v1), the MCP endpoint, the Verifier's /api/attest, and /api/status. All of it ran locally against mock upstreams with injected latency. Nothing here was measured on Vercel or against real Helius or Blob, so treat the absolute numbers as a lower bound on latency. What carries over to production is the shape: how many upstream calls each request makes, what the caches absorb, and where the limits bite.

Method#

  • Harness (bench/harness.mjs): one Node process serves the real handlers, the way a Vercel function instance runs them: api/v1/index.js, api/mcp.js, api/status/index.js, and the Verifier bundle's attest and log routes.
  • Upstreams, all injected and counted.
    • Solana RPC (bench/rpc.mjs, a separate process): 30 ms per call. It holds 5,000 certificates (a third each wallet, X and badge subjects; a tenth revoked; a quarter USDC), 50 rooms of 20 members, and Config and Treasury.
    • Rings indexer: 60 ms per call. The fake Rings source is built with the Helius SDK itself (the Verifier's test doubles), and its Merkle paths are precomputed so the fake does not dominate.
    • Vercel Blob: 40 ms per operation, on a filesystem store.
    • The transparency log: 2,000 entries, each signed with the key the mock Config lists.
  • Load (bench/load.mjs): a k6-style closed-loop generator with no dependencies. 20 virtual users each send their next request as soon as the last one answers, for 10 s. Every latency is recorded, so percentiles are exact. One warm request (or warm-until-200) runs before each scenario.
  • Upstream accounting: the counters are reset before each scenario. Afterwards "rpc/req" is the RPC calls divided by requests, "other/req" is Blob and indexer operations, and "cpu/req" is the harness process's CPU time divided by requests.
  • Memo on / off: /v1 keeps a short per-instance memo of heavy reads. "Memo off" disables it and shows the cost of a cold instance, or of a cache miss.
  • Bursts (bench/burst.mjs): many requests at once, the production MCP limits, and cold caches.
  • Cold start (bench/coldstart.mjs): a fresh Node process per run, median of 5. It times the import of each function module, then its first request.
  • Machine: Apple M2 Pro (10 cores), 16 GB, Node 20.20.2. The machine was shared with other build agents: the 1/5/15-minute load average was 49/32/30 during the final run. Numbers under this load are pessimistic.

Reproduce: node bench/run.mjs (add --quick for 4 s scenarios), node bench/burst.mjs, node bench/coldstart.mjs. Raw results are in bench/results/: 23-14-58 is the baseline, before most fixes; 23-46-32, burst-… and coldstart-… are final.

How to read it:

  • A subject lookup costs one RPC call (getMultipleAccounts, with the slot taken from its context). Its latency is the RPC's latency plus about 1 ms. The 2 to 4 PDAs it derives are cached.
  • Lists, rooms and stats are memoized for 5 to 10 s per instance, and the memo is single-flight. On a hit they cost well under 1 ms of CPU. On a miss, the certificate scan is the one expensive read: about 100 ms of CPU to decode 5,000 accounts, and a getProgramAccounts returning about 3.5 MB of base64. Stats on a miss makes 7 RPC calls: the scan, Config, Treasury, the verifier key history, and the log head.
  • Log proofs cost the log nothing per request. Each instance mirrors the log, checks that the mirror's root equals the signed head, and computes the audit path itself.
  • Attest makes 6 indexer calls and 7 Blob operations, and uses 13 ms of CPU per signature. Its latency is the upstream chain. The baseline run had 15 operations and a 1 s p50; the Verifier has since made some of them parallel.

Found in other parts (reported to the lead)#

  1. Verifier log rate limit vs server-side readers. log:<ip> (burst 120, then 60 per minute) sees every server-side reader on the deployment as one client. Agreed fix: an x-pof-internal header from env, which /v1 and the witness now send; the status page sends it too.
  2. Verifier range(from, to) read one Blob object per entry, one after another: a 256-entry page took 256 × 40 ms. A cold mirror of the 1,000-entry log took about 10 s in an earlier run. In the final run the cold burst was served (1,040 Blob reads for 1,000 entries), so the reads now overlap. A cold mirror still costs one Blob read per log entry, on every new instance. Storing the log in 256-entry chunks would make that L/256.
  3. Attest's upstream chain is sequential in places. It went from 15 to 13 operations and from a 1 s to a 385 ms p50 during this round.
  4. /api/status cachedRun was not single-flight: 200 cold requests made 660 RPC calls. That was fixed upstream during the round (now 4).

N certificates a day. Issuing costs 6N indexer calls and 7N Blob operations: N = 1,000 is 6,000 indexer calls and 7,000 Blob ops a day. Add the relayer's transactions for handle and badge subjects. Reading them is bounded by the edge cache rather than by N:

  • every lookup URL is cached for 5 s at each edge region (s-maxage=5), so one subject costs at most one RPC call per 5 s per region however popular it is;
  • 100,000 lookups a day spread over many subjects cost at most 100,000 getMultipleAccounts calls (1.2 a second).

Where the limits are.

  • Lists and stats grow with the total number of certificates. At 50,000 certificates a scan is about 35 MB of base64 and about 1 s of CPU. Before that size:
    • serve the wall from an index the keeper or witness keeps (Blob), not from getProgramAccounts;
    • keep the default filters, so the edge and the memo stay effective.
  • A cold instance's log mirror reads the whole log once. That is one Blob read per entry on the Verifier's side, and about 64 bytes of hashes plus up to 15 MB of cached leaves per 100,000 entries in memory.
  • Helius: lookups are standard calls; getProgramAccounts is the heavy one, and the indexer calls are per certificate. Map these counts onto the plan's credit table in the Helius dashboard. Under the current plan, set the daily spend alert from audit POF-14.
  • Vercel: function CPU is about 1 ms per warm read and about 13 ms per attest. A million reads a month is under half a CPU-hour. Invocation count and egress of the larger list answers matter more than CPU. The Firewall rate rules (OWNER-STEPS devx 4) cap what one caller can spend.

Not measured#

  • Real network to Helius and Blob, the edge cache, TLS and cold Vercel regions.
  • The relayer's on-chain transactions.
  • The browser client's proving time.
  • Concurrent writers to the real Blob store (the Verifier's create-if-absent log appends were tested on the filesystem store only).
